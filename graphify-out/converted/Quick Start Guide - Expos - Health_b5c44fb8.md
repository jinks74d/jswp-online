| STEP ONE:
GATHERING CDS

LIST AND ADD 3-5 concrete details (CDs), but you may also add as many as you like. You may click <Add CD> or touch “Enter” on the keyboard to add CDs.
HIGHLIGHT the two or more lines of CDs that you want to use for your chunk. Remember, for expository (nonliterary), the ratio is 2+:1, so this step helps you choose your CDs.
The red buttons allow you to drag and drop CDs in the order you would like them to appear.
PRINT and/or CLICK <Save and Next>. |  |
| STEP TWO:
WORKING TOPIC SENTENCE

REVIEW the writing prompt.
TURN the prompt around to compose a “working topic sentence.”
After you type several characters, you’ll notice that the CDs you selected on the previous page populate in the order you chose. |  |

| STEP THREE:
COMMENTARY DEVELOPMENT

REVIEW your first CD. ASK YOURSELF, “Why is this important?”
BRAINSTORM your ideas, starting with the middle oval.
CONTINUE BRAINSTORMING. You must provide at least three commentary (CM) ideas.
REVIEW your second CD. FOLLOW STEPS #2-4 above to generate commentary ideas for your second CD.
Note: the auto-fill is for 3+:0.
PRINT and/or CLICK <Save and Next>. |  |

STEP FOUR:
THE SHAPING SHEET

PROOF, EDIT, AND REVISE your topic sentence (TS). Our original TS had a “to be” verb. Action verbs give more information and have more of an impact. One way to rid your sentence of a “to be” verb is to invert the sentence.


We now need to write our concrete detail sentences. Use a transition when moving from the first CD to the second one. Here, we use “in addition.”



We liked our commentary sentence (CM), but we realized that in our original concluding sentence, we were repeating “these” and “actions;” so, we’re going to make some changes.


We revise our concluding sentence (CS) to avoid the repetitive words we mentioned above. Also, we fix the subject/verb agreement error from “leads” to “lead.”

| The Shaping Sheet: Revising, Editing, and Proofing to Produce a Second Draft

Revising, editing, and proofing is the final step before your final draft.
For the TS, notice how we added the author’s name, the title of the short story. We answered the prompt and situated the answer by telling the reader this is how Cecilia feels at the beginning of the story.
For the CD, we embedded “slowly,” “wandered,” and “flopped,” into a sentence by starting with a ”lead-in,” paraphrasing what was happening right before. Notice how we revised the quotations into the literary present tense.
For the first CM, we didn’t want to keep repeating “Cecilia,” so we described her – this teenager. We also placed “her” in front of “time” and “life’s decisions.”
For the second CM, we decided we could start with a noun clause instead of repeating her name again.
We kept the TS the same. We liked it as is.
